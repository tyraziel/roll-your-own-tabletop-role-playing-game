Have You Ever Played Pretend?

Maybe you've played house, had a tea party, run a pretend restaurant, or played cops and robbers. Maybe you've dressed up as someone else or made up adventures with dolls, action figures, or Little People.

Guess what? You've already been role-playing!

When you pretend to be someone else, decide what they do, and make up what happens next, you're role-playing.

Playing Pretend Can Look Like...

Running a food truck or restaurant
Playing cops and robbers
Being a chef, builder, or shopkeeper
Running a pretend grocery market
Playing with dolls, a dollhouse, or Little People
Dressing up as a hero or another character

So What Makes It a Tabletop Role-Playing Game?

It's playing pretend together, with a few extra tools to help everyone share the story:

  1. Characters: Who are you pretending to be?
  2. A world: Where does your adventure happen?
  3. A story: What are you trying to do?
  4. Challenges and rules: How do you decide what happens when something is difficult or uncertain?
  5. A Game Master: Someone who describes the world and helps guide what happens next.

But What If You Disagree?

One player says, “I caught you!”
Another player says, “No, you didn't!”

In pretend play, you might just decide together. In a tabletop role-playing game, you can also make rules or roll dice to help decide what happens next.

The dice help determine what happens. The Game Master decides what that result means in the story.

Today, you're the game designer! You'll invent your own characters, world, challenges, and rules—then try playing the game you created.

Game Master tip: Start by asking, “What do you want to do?” Then let the story grow from the answer.
